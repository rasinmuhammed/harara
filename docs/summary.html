Project summary · Doha

Forecast-driven heat-safety analysis for outdoor labour

An evaluation of whether public forecast data supports better heat-safety decision-making for Gulf outdoor workers, and two proof-of-concept contributions. Full methodology and results are in the technical report; this page is a summary.

Context

The regulatory baseline

Migrant construction and delivery workers in the Gulf are exposed to a humid-heat hazard linked to excess cardiovascular mortality (Pradhan et al., 2019) and, for gig workers, flagged by Human Rights Watch and a 2024 ILO convention. Qatar Ministerial Decision 17/2021 mandates a fixed midday outdoor-work ban (about 10:00–15:30, 1 June to 15 September) and a stop-work clause at WBGT above 32.1 °C.

On the 16-year record, WBGT exceeds 32.1 °C on 58 to 61% of hours in the regulated summer working window, and the annual exceedance-hour count is trending up.

Forecast evaluation

Four hypotheses, all rejected

Each was tested with walk-forward evaluation, station adjudication, and block-bootstrap intervals. See the results ledger for the full list of 18 questions.

Finding

The calendar ban is too narrow

Applying the ACGIH TLV screening criteria to the 16-year WBGT record: the ban covers 25% of daylight warm-season hours. For heavy work by unacclimatised workers, 1085 hours per year of physiologically unsafe conditions fall outside the ban window — 57% of the hours it leaves open, and 63% of all such unsafe daylight hours. Over-restriction (hours the ban closes that are in fact safe) is about 6% for light acclimatised work and near zero for anything heavier.

Contribution 2

Individual heat-strain estimation (proof of concept)

A particle filter with a two-node thermoregulation process model, forced by the measured environment and an accelerometry-derived metabolic rate, estimating individual core temperature from heart rate and a skin-temperature patch. It self-calibrates to the person through a latent metabolic scale, and propagates the particle cloud forward under the WBGT forecast to give the probability of core temperature exceeding 38.5 °C within a horizon.

Further work

Next steps

  • The wearable pilot: 30 participants over at least 4 sessions, ingestible-capsule ground truth, a mixed-effects Bland–Altman primary endpoint, and sensor-ablation and acclimatisation secondary endpoints.
  • Completing the GEFS v12 reforecast backfill (2000–2019) and refreshing the ensemble calibration and forecast-reliability results on the full record.
  • A targeted ERA5 cross-check of the forecast-evaluation findings.
  • Instrumented sites for block-scale microclimate, which the spatial and extreme-day analyses show public products cannot resolve.

Method basis: Liljegren WBGT (thermofeel); ACGIH TLV screening (ISO 7243); the two-node Gagge–Stolwijk–Nishi model; Rockafellar–Uryasev CVaR optimisation; a bootstrap particle filter. Station observations (OTHH METAR) are used as the reference throughout and identified a wind-data defect in the reanalysis archive from November 2024, since patched.
