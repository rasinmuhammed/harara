## Summary

Rejected: bias-correcting NWP WBGT; metro-scale spatial downscaling;
regime-conditional gridded failure; forecasts failing specifically on the
dangerous days; AI weather models degrading humid-heat stop-work decisions
(the opposite holds -- NOAA GFS WBGT misses about 40% of exceedance hours at
all leads while ECMWF IFS and AIFS miss 6 to 15%).

Supported: the calendar ban leaves about 60% of the role-specific danger
uncovered.

Found, sized and applied: a second archive defect, by cross-checking against
two independent products (rows 34, 35). ERA5 corroborates the Open-Meteo wind
defect a second way and agrees strongly with the working WBGT series overall.
It also disagreed with the working dataset on the size of the section 4.1
rising-trend claim; triangulating that disagreement against METAR (the
measured station) found a second, previously undocumented Open-Meteo defect
- summer humidity drifted from a wet bias to a persistent dry bias in 2018
and stayed dry ever since - and resolved it in ERA5's direction. Unlike the
wind defect, this one was applied: `data/doha_wbgt_16yr.csv` was rebuilt on
the corrected record, and the reported near-flat exceedance-hour trend
becomes a clearly rising one (+9.0 hours/year, close to the sizing estimate
and 59% of ERA5's independent figure). Every study that depends on this file
was re-run against the corrected version (rows 12c, 12d, 14a, 15, 18); none
reversed, but two moved by more than rounding: the scheduler's peak-load gap
versus calendar shrank (row 14a, 44% to 31% on the analog sample), and the
stochastic-vs-deterministic hedging result weakened to non-significant at
two of three leads on analog scenarios while holding on GEFS's own ensemble
(row 15 vs row 12d) - a real, sample-dependent sensitivity, not resolved by
this pass, and now documented rather than hidden behind a single number.

Rejected on re-examination (row 14a): risk-optimal daily scheduling does not
beat the calendar rule once the crew's time on site, day fragmentation and
cumulative heat dose are constrained. The earlier 14% peak-load reduction came
from spreading the same work across a longer on-site day, which for a
bussed-in accommodation worker is a cost, not a saving. Held to a short span
the optimiser runs hotter than the calendar rule; a plain earlier start is
better on heat but under-delivers work on about half of peak-season days. The
safety gains that hold are structural (which hours are workable at all,
acclimatisation, shelter, an earlier start where feasible); the daily layer's
job is to be never worse for the worker than the enforceable rule.

Partly supported on real physiology (PROSPIE, rectal reference): the physics
filter with a skin-temperature channel beats the heart-rate-only state of the
art on real bodies, RMSE 0.41 vs 0.52 C, bias near zero. The synthetic 0.083 C
MAE does not transfer (real best 0.35 C), and the filter's credible intervals
are not calibrated out of domain. A pilot in the target population is still
required.

Built, deterministic core intact, and now scored against a real model: a
language-model interface layer that parses requests, extracts cited rule
constraints, and writes grounded briefings without producing any number itself.
With K2-Horizon driving it, the fail-closed properties hold (every ambiguous
request asks back, every injected foreign number is caught by the guard);
extraction quality is uneven and the model is not reproducible at temperature
zero. See rows 20 and 21.

Open, and requiring the pilot: anticipatory individual heat-strain estimation
against measured core temperature; block-scale microclimate modelling.

Scored on the full 2010-2019 truth overlap (rows 12c, 12d, 18): EMOS
calibration cuts GEFS WBGT CRPS by 30 to 33% and corrects a roughly 2 to 3x
under-dispersed raw ensemble; with that honestly-spread ensemble, stochastic
(CVaR) scheduling is confirmed worse than the deterministic point-forecast LP,
more clearly than the earlier analog-scenario result; and the reliability
classifier shows a real but modest, not yet operational, lift over predicting
forecast surprise from a spread rule alone. The 2000-2009 part of the GEFS
backfill (before the WBGT truth series starts) does not change any of these
three results and is not required for them.
